# Hatches

**Status: built; archived 2026-10-12.** The external-effects record
([2026-10-03](../decisions/2026-10-03-external-effects.md)) built the one hatch that carries a record;
this decides what it shares with the others, what the compiler owes each, and which words
an author writes at all.

## The question

The platform already asks one question everywhere, under different names: a retried
form, a CDC redelivery, a second clock, a crashed worker's re-entry, a time-travel replay
are all *a second delivery*. `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, `Idempotency-Key`,
`uuid5(schedule, instant)`, `resolution=ignore-duplicates`, the `refused` arrow, the
re-entry ladder are all answers to it. This record names the question — **what happens
if it happens twice** — and derives from it the three views an app's impurity has:
where the row is, where the code sits, and what delivery it can claim.

## Built foundation

What the schema holds, in the order one thinks about it ([schema.cue](../../schema.cue),
[machine.cue](https://github.com/bonisoft3/omnishell/blob/v0.19.1/machine.cue), [objects.ts](../../objects.ts)).

**Entities.** `#Entity` is five things that matter — `durability`, `fields`, `access`
(server rows only), `validations`, `seed` — plus the derived `read`, `writers` and
`level`, and a long tail of schema evolution (`id`, `ordinal`, `retired`, `generated`,
`indexes`, `uniques`, `invariant`, `cel`). Everything that decides a row's tier, roles,
read mode and level follows from `durability`.

**ir.html.** Thirteen kinds (`objects.ts` `KINDS`): `entity`, `pipeline`, `screen`,
`state`, `flow`, `test`, `decision`, `handler`, `validation`, `hatch`, `unit`, and since
this record's second slice `machine` and `effect`. Presence is by reach ([review by
consequence](../decisions/2026-08-27-review-by-consequence.md), built here): a `tab` entity, its
validations, its charts and the handlers only they name are machine-checked and owe no
element. `state` is a screen's lifecycle wireframe, not a chart's state.

**Machines.** One chart shape, two interpreters. The terminal's
([machines.md](https://github.com/bonisoft3/omnishell/blob/v0.19.1/docs/machines.md)): a transition is
`{guard?, target?, assign?, effect?: {op, entity, …}, raise?}`. The cluster's
(`#MechaMachine`): `{on?, after?, invoke?, entry?, exit?}` with actions
`{assign?, effect?, raise?}`, run inside the write by a `BEFORE` trigger. A chart states
no level: the terminal's lint reads it off the entity the effect writes (slice 1); the
cluster's effects carry none, being writes to `server` rows by construction. SQL function calls name a `sql` hatch; `notify` (`pg_notify`) and `stream`
(a signal to a pipeline) are raises. An effect
declaration (`state.effects`) freezes an `input`; its `entity` is derived, the one
machine whose state names it by `invoke.src`.

## The axis: the level is a projection of durability

Durability is not one fact but a bundle, of which exactly five combinations are
meaningful; the level is the one component the rest of the platform shares:

| durability | level | persists past the tab | how it is read | `access` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tab` | 1 ephemeral | no | local | — |
| `device` | 1 ephemeral | yes | local | — |
| `offline` | 2 compensable | — | a cached copy | required |
| `live` | 2 compensable | — | live | required |
| `server` | 3 replicated | — | never by the terminal | required |

So the level derives from durability and not the other way round: `ephemeral` does not
say tab from device, `compensable` does not say offline from live, and the terminal needs
those bits. The effect levels of [REFERENCE.md](https://github.com/bonisoft3/omnishell/blob/v0.19.1/REFERENCE.md#effects)
are durability extended by one rung at each end — *no row* below, *a row nobody here
holds* above — and each rung is one answer to the question:

| level | the row written lives | if it happens twice | formal model | the obligation, and who discharges it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 projection | nowhere | nothing: it is a function | a function | no ambient authority — `jessie.ts`, the compartment |
| 1 ephemeral | `tab`, `device` | the same register, one writer | a Mealy machine over an event log the owner holds | the log replays — the battery, the Postman tour |
| 2 compensable | `offline`, `live` | the server may refuse; the speculative row is withdrawn, never undone by arithmetic | optimistic concurrency with abort-only compensation (Kung & Robinson 1981); the saga's compensatable step | the chart draws `refused` — `machineLint` |
| 3 replicated | `server` | a join: `x ⊔ x = x`; out of order, it commutes | a join-semilattice (Shapiro et al. 2011; CALM, Hellerstein & Alvaro 2020); inside the trigger, a serializable transaction; the saga's retriable step | a lattice op on the feed; `UNIQUE` in the transaction |
| 4 external | outside the cluster | the peer answers: by key, by query, by a human, or by accepting the duplicate | durable execution: a deterministic function replayed over a history of recorded outcomes; the saga's pivot | the ledger claims before the call; the re-entry is declared |

Two algebras, then, and an axis of ownership. Level 0 is functions. Levels 1–4 are all
join-semilattices, differing in whose lattice and where it lives: a register you alone
write; the server's lattice with your speculative rows as an overlay (`refused` is the
overlay being dropped, which is why compensation never subtracts); the server's lattice
itself, where `ensure` and `upsert` are joins and `accumulate` is one only when keyed by
the event; and the ledger, a grow-only set of attempts (`ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` is
union), which is what turns an act into a level-3 row while the peer stays opaque.
"Twice" is one law, idempotence of join; "out of order" is its sibling, commutativity;
replay by recompute is CALM: a monotone program converges from any delivery order.

**Below the hatch the platform is monotone.** The hatch is where monotonicity stops — a
sent mail cannot be un-joined — and the ledger is how the *attempt* is pushed back into
the monotone world even when the peer is not. Composition is lattice product: a machine
over several entities lives in the product of their lattices, held where the farthest one
is held, so a chart's level is the `max` over the durabilities its arrows write and a
screen's or an app's is the same fold.

**The level is never authored.** It is top of the taxonomy and bottom of the authoring,
for one reason: a write's level is `f(where the row lives, where the writer stands)`, and
both are declared already, so an authored level can only agree (noise) or disagree (a
bug the compiler cannot tell from intent). Claiming less (`live` as `ephemeral`) skips the
`refused` arrow while the server can still say no; claiming more (`live` as `replicated`)
calls a speculative write converged — which is what golaberto's charts did, and the only
thing the label achieved was dodging the lint. The one legitimate variation, a browser
write that is convergent because its op is a keyed join, follows from the op, another
declared fact. What the level deserves is to be *visible*: a derived field on the entity
and the machine, shown in ir.html and said in every lint sentence ("compensable (live)").

Level 3 is one level, not two. Inside the trigger's transaction atomicity absorbs the
duplicate, so `create` strict is admitted; on the change feed delivery is at-least-once
and unordered, so only joins are. The platform already enforces half of this unnamed —
crud adds `Prefer: resolution=ignore-duplicates` to every pipeline write
([proxy](https://github.com/bonisoft3/mecha/blob/v0.17.1/docs/proxy.md)) — and the other half is one lint: a
pipeline admits `ensure`, `upsert` and keyed `accumulate`; strict `create` and unkeyed
`update` are the trigger's. Helland's ACID 2.0 (2009) is the reference for why; it is
not a level.

**Sources are the inbound face of the same axis.** A question may be asked twice; an act
may not be performed twice (command–query separation, Meyer 1988). A question is keyed by **content** and its
**memo** — Michie's memo table (1968): answers addressed by what was asked — makes the
second ask free; an act is keyed by **identity** and its **ledger** holds the attempt.
The clock, a click, a schedule's cron and an engine's answer all land as rows of an
append-only set. A source read *inside* a write — `clock_timestamp()`, `gen_random_uuid()`
in a default, `{now}` in an assign — memoizes itself in the column it fills, which is why
a replayed trigger is still deterministic: it reads the row, not the clock.

## Code: isolation, ports, adapters

Code has no level. The level belongs to the row a write lands on, and app code never
writes: it describes, and the platform performs — the terminal's interpreter at 1–2,
mecha's interpreter (the `BEFORE` trigger; "guard" is XState's word for a condition and
is retired for the trigger) at 3, the airlock at 4. The proposed classification specifies code by isolation and ports; its
level follows the rows its requests reach. The built unit grammar is
[the terminal's contract](https://github.com/bonisoft3/omnishell/blob/v0.19.1/docs/terminal.md).

**Isolation** — how far it can reach *without* the platform, named as protection domains
(Saltzer & Schroeder 1975) by the web platform's own rungs, each adding exactly one
authority:

| `isolation` | the mechanism | adds | may be |
|---|---|---|---|
| `compartment` | SES, same realm | nothing: no ambient authority | level 0 only — every Jessie role sits here implicitly; a WASI job in the compute cage is the same rung for native code |
| `realm` | an iframe: own globals and DOM (ECMA-262 *realm*) | presenting | a presenter: shows foreign content, raises events; same-site, no storage, no network beyond the app |
| `agent` | a worker: own thread and event loop (ECMA-262 *agent*) | the network, IndexedDB | a **question**: it asks and its answers land in a memo; an agent that acts on a third party is refused, since an act from N tabs is N acts and acts are server-only structurally. Without a memo it is a presenter whose worker only paints ([the seats](https://github.com/bonisoft3/omnishell/blob/v0.19.1/docs/terminal.md#the-seats), [[../decisions/2026-10-22-endowed-babylon]]) |
| `process` | a container | the operating system | an actor on the world: a hatch |

The airlock is the platform's own `process`, whose ports are the ledger and the declared
binding ([the seats](https://github.com/bonisoft3/omnishell/blob/v0.19.1/docs/terminal.md#the-seats)).

**Ports** — everything it touches, as entities and events (ports and adapters, Cockburn
2005; Erlang's ports to code outside the VM; the component model's world of imports and
exports): `reads` (rows handed in), `raises` (events to charts, which then write at the
entity's level), and for a `process` `writes` (the role it connects as and the tables its
grants reach; mecha's interpreter still runs the chart on them, since the trigger is
`SECURITY DEFINER`). The platform's side of a port is an **adapter** — the `postMessage`
bridge, the Dapr binding, the crud role — which is why the Jessie `adapters` role has the
name it has.

## Delivery, derived

The question's answer has a standard name — delivery semantics, as stream processors
classify their sinks (Flink: idempotent, transactional, at-least-once) — and it is
derived, never declared:

| `delivery` | at level | derived from |
|---|---|---|
| `exactly-once` | 0–3; 4 with `idempotent` or `reconcile` | a function; one writer; a join; the key or the query |
| `at-most-once` | 2; 4 with `unknown` | the `refused` arrow; a human closes it |
| `at-least-once` | 4 with `repeat` | the duplicate is accepted, for the reason the `note` states |
| `unspecified` | a hatch | nobody answers; the hatch's `note` is the reason it is admitted |

At level 3 the op decides, and the vocabulary is one, the terminal's
`#Machine.#MutationOp` ([machine.cue](https://github.com/bonisoft3/omnishell/blob/v0.19.1/machine.cue)), each word
classified there by what happens if it happens twice:

| op | twice | delivery |
|---|---|---|
| `ensure`, `upsert`, `delete`, absolute `update` | a join: once | exactly-once |
| `create` | a duplicate, unless the client minted the key, which the terminal does: a retry carries the same key and is a join on the wire (crud's `ignore-duplicates`, above); `data-sync.js` `add` runs the entity's validations as an insert, nothing more | exactly-once in the trigger's transaction; on the feed only by its key |
| `accumulate` | adds twice | exactly-once only as a fold over a keyed event set |
| `reduce` | whatever the ops it returns do | theirs |
| `navigate` | writes no row: it fills the route's params, and a second hash change lands on the same route | exactly-once |

The level-4 re-entry ladder is delivery spelled out, and the level names are the saga's
(Garcia-Molina & Salem 1987): `compensable` is the compensatable step, `replicated` the
retriable one (a join may be re-run freely), `external` the pivot — the point of no
return, which is why it alone needs the ledger. `ephemeral` is the local step every saga
paper leaves implicit.

## Hatches

**A hatch is a performer the platform did not build**: an app-authored artifact that
reaches past level 0 without the platform performing the crossing, so its delivery is the
author's to answer or nobody's. The platform owes it nothing on replay — it runs as
declared — and that is what distinguishes it from an effect, which carries a record. A
hatch is declared like everything else: what it is, what it touches, and a `note`; no
field beside `note` says why, since `note` is already that.

| | reaches | delivery | record | replay | declares |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sql`, `proto` | level 3, *inspection withheld* (squawk, buf cannot read it) | unspecified | none | re-runs it | `files`, `note`; refused if it loads an extension that does I/O — that is level 4 wearing 3's clothes |
| a raw pipeline (`#Pipeline.src`, the whole rpk stream the emitter cannot see inside) | level 3 on the feed; level 4 if an output leaves the cluster | unspecified | none | runs as declared | `note`; an http output is a `process` hatch's reach and is declared as one |
| `process` (today `container`) | level 4 in the cluster | unspecified | none | runs as declared | `ports.writes` (role, tables), `egress`, `note` |
| an external effect | level 4 | derived from its re-entry | the ledger | answers from the ledger | not a hatch: the platform performs it |
| `repeat` | level 4, at-least-once | declared | the ledger and the `note` | answers from the ledger | an effect whose `note` admits the duplicate |
| a `host-dom` module | ambient host DOM, browser APIs and app client | unspecified | none | remounts with its screen | `src`, `files`, non-empty `note`; named by the screen, no isolation |
| a vendored `agent` | the browser's network | `exactly-once` as a question | a memo | answers from it | `memo`, `ports`; not a hatch while it only asks |
| a vendored `realm`, `compartment` | present and raise | derived from the chart handling its events | none | re-runs | `ports`; never a hatch |
| `call` (plpgsql) | whatever the function does | unspecified | none | runs as declared | **a `sql` hatch**, not an effect |
| `notify`, `stream` | a port | — | — | — | **raises**, not effects |
| inline `<script>`, `on*=`, `javascript:`, a static with code | unbounded, undeclared | none | none | — | **refused**: the markup rule and the door's `Content-Security-Policy` with `require-trusted-types-for 'script'` are the fence that makes "only four rungs exist" true at runtime; `connect-src`, `img-src` and `font-src` close the question a stylesheet or an image URL could ask of a foreign origin |

The markup/CSP fence is built; explicit remote-resource declarations preserve
remote presentation where an app requires it ([[../../SPEC|the contract]]); `process` remains `hatches.kind:
"container"`, without authored ports or egress in the hatch declaration.

Tests are outside the axis: a test is not a delivery and never happens twice in
production. Platform code is the performer, not a hatch; its correctness is the
platform's own suite.

## What is authored, and what is derived

The criterion: **authored is a fact the compiler cannot know; derived is everything that
follows.** Every authored word names a fact about the world, not about the program.

| authored | a fact about | borrowed from | derives |
|---|---|---|---|
| `durability` | where the row lives | — (it bundles level, persistence, read mode) | level, tier, roles, read mode, ir presence by reach |
| `access` | who may see or write | RLS; object capabilities | policies |
| a **mutation**'s `op` (`#MutationOp`, the terminal's, defined once in `machine.cue`; a chart narrows it to `create\|update\|delete\|upsert`, mecha's `#RelationalOp` to `create\|ensure\|upsert\|accumulate\|update\|delete`, a form's action and a `#Write` are its words too) | the write's algebra | GraphQL's mutation; CQRS's command; CRDTs for which ops are joins | delivery at level 3; what a pipeline may contain |
| the **sink** (`origin\|bridge`, `idempotent`, `reentry`) | where the act lands and how that peer behaves | dataflow's source/sink; Flink's sink guarantees; durable execution | level 4, delivery, the ledger |
| `isolation` + `ports` (+ `memo` for an `agent`) | how far code is trusted, what it touches, where its answers land | protection domains; ECMA-262 realms and agents; ports and adapters; the memo table | the rung's ceiling, question or act, the unit's level |
| `note` | the judgment, on every declaration | — | — |

Derived, never authored: the level, delivery, replay, the effect's entity (that of the
one machine that invokes it, and no field of the effect), and the entity's `writers` and
`read`, since every writer is a declaration and every read is in the markup.

The live contracts are [[../../SPEC|the artifact spec]] and
[the terminal's machines](https://github.com/bonisoft3/omnishell/blob/v0.19.1/docs/machines.md). The following
excerpt names the fields the chess agent declares; `files` also names its
pinned binary, licence and notice.

```cue
capabilities: vendored: stockfish: {
	isolation: "agent"
	src: "shell/units/stockfish/unit.js"
	ports: {reads: ["Game"], raises: ["answer"]}
	memo: {entity: "Answer", key: ["fen", "nodes", "lines"]}
	files: [
		"shell/units/stockfish/unit.js",
		"shell/units/stockfish/stockfish-18-lite-single.js",
		"shell/units/stockfish/stockfish-18-lite-single.wasm",
		"shell/units/stockfish/LICENSE",
		"shell/units/stockfish/NOTICE.md",
	]
	note: "the pinned engine answers a preference, which the referee checks against Legal"
}
```

`shape`, a common `#Reach` projection and `isolation: "process"` are
proposals, absent from the grammar. Inspection exemptions still declare
`hatches.kind: "container" | "sql" | "proto" | "host-dom"`; units declare `vendored`.
The XState DSL is constrained: a terminal chart has no `effect.level` or
`context`, an external effect freezes `input`, and `invoke` remains the
machine's actor reference.

## One fact, said once: unifications between entity, machine and effect

Reading the three grammars side by side, several facts are stated twice. XState's own
structure says which copy is primary.

1. **`machine.initial` ⇔ the status column's `default`.** Built: the machine
   supplies a missing SQL default and a differing authored
   default is refused. With no field CEL the states supply a CHECK; a closed CEL
   enum must admit every state. A seed's status must be a state of the machine.
2. **A chart's `context` ⇔ the row; `seed` ⇔ XState's `input`.** Built: a chart
   declares no `context`; the entity owns its fields and their
   defaults, and the chart owns `initial`. Keeping a second initial row under the
   chart would leave two statements of every field's default. An explicit
   `data-empty-row` remains a whole row supplied by markup.
3. **`validations` ⇔ the guards of the entity's lifecycle machine.** Built:
   lifecycle transitions select named Jessie guards and relational actions.
   The entity's validations are derived from those guards. The server checks
   them in its AFTER interpreter and the browser in its mutation preflight.
   Access remains RLS; [[2026-10-12-lifecycle-guards|the decision]]
   explains why authorization precedes privileged guard reads.
4. **`after` on a `server` state ⇔ a one-shot `schedule` row.** A browser chart's timer
   has the terminal's clock; a timer on a server row has nothing to fire it when no write
   arrives — the problem the airlock's wake solved with `due_at` rows re-armed by the
   trigger. Entering the state arms the row; the ticker's sweep raises the event. Same
   table, same wake, no second clock.
5. **An entity's `read` and `writers` are derived.** `read: eager` is required exactly
   when a screen reads the table whole (`data-sync.js` throws otherwise); `writers` is
   which forms, charts, machines, pipelines and computations name it, each with its
   ops. Authored `ports` remain only on a vendored unit and a `process`, where the
   compiler cannot see inside.

## What ir.html shows

**Presence is by reach**, the rule [review by consequence](../decisions/2026-08-27-review-by-consequence.md)
decided and built in slice 2: a `tab` entity is machine-checked only; `device` or above owes
an element, since the row reaches another tab or a later session — and the URL, minimal
in durability and unbounded in reach, is read as a separate axis there. The same rule
read through the fold decides a machine: it owes an element iff the highest durability
its arrows write is `device` or above — and a handler, whose reach is its chart's, the
same. The program claim's `owed` field carries that reach.
Chess's four selector charts on `Game` each owe one; the renderer makes that cheap. An
effect always does.

Two kinds join the eleven: `machine` and `effect`, each showing its derived level and
delivery. **The compiler draws, the author writes prose.** `write.ts` emits the
entity's and the machine's diagrams from `program.cue`, deterministic and pinned, and the
`data-kind` element embeds them; the picture is then the bijection checker's, never stale.
Which renderer is measured, not assumed: Mermaid 11 with `layout: elk` (already pinned in
every ir.html head; `realworld` draws a `flowchart` by hand) against D2 (`sql_table` for
entities, ELK layout, themes; a Go binary pinned through mise, SVG at build time), on the
same three objects — golaberto's `Game` neighbourhood, the payment machine, chess's
metronome — judged side by side. The renderer must produce the SVG at build time with no
browser in the loop, which D2 does natively and Mermaid only through a headless Chromium;
that constraint may decide the measurement before the eye does. The measurement is
[kept beside this record](../decisions/evidence/hatches-renderers/README.md): the sources, both
renderings of each object, and what the run established without an eye. One ordering fact: ir.html
is authored before `program.cue` exists, so the author places the `data-kind` element and
names the picture (`ir/machine-<id>.svg`), and `write.ts` fills it when the program is
there — the bijection then checks both the element and the file. The app's summary is
derived by the fold — its level, a count per delivery class (the classes are not totally
ordered: at-most-once and at-least-once fail differently), its hatches — so an app's whole
impurity is reviewable on one line.

**The entity's element is an HTML card, not a drawing.** The measurement's second half
settled the split: a graph renderer draws arrows well and forms badly, and a card has
almost no arrows in it. So the card is HTML, styled from the platform's own tokens, and
only the machine inside it and the neighbourhood around it are D2. Its conventions are
Azimutt's, the one existing tool whose job is exploring a schema by clicking on it:
**the card is the navigation** — referrers on its left edge, references on its right, each
a link; constraint **glyphs** on the column rows (pk · → ref · ◇ unique · ≡ indexed · ○
nullable) with the CEL rule and default beside, never words; the platform's **stamp
columns** (`created_by`, `updated_by`) folded into the derived section, which also shrinks
the neighbourhood diagram; a durability **band** on the edge; and a collapsed state of pk,
refs and the machine's field — Azimutt's "related columns only". Authored sections keep a
solid ink rule, derived ones a dashed accent rule. Four rounds on the judged page settled
the card's contract: every fact is a chip reading `property: value` (`durability: server`,
`access: public`, then dashed `level: replicated`, `delivery: exactly-once`; on the derived
line `writers:`, `read:`, `roles:`, `ir:`), each explaining itself in one sentence on hover
or focus, so the words `replicated` and `exactly-once` never stand alone; the authored chips
sit together in the header and the derived ones on their own line; every field name is
upfront on one line with an ellipsis, marked only where a mark is navigation (pk, →, the
machine's field), the metadata one click below with a legend; every list longer than a
glance is behind its count (`+8`, `all fields`); and the machine is the one picture. A screen's `reads` are a **layout** in
Azimutt's sense, so each screen owes a neighbourhood diagram of exactly those entities and
the app's is their union. What only makes sense interactively — find a path between two
entities, search across notes and constraints — is the argument for ir.html one day being
an omnishell app ([pronto written in pronto](../decisions/2026-09-27-pronto-written-in-pronto.md)).

## References at the point of use

Every formalism this record leans on is named where the code uses it, once, in one line:
the saga steps at `#DurabilityEffectLevel`; Kung & Robinson at the `refused` rule;
Shapiro at `#MutationOp`, CALM at `#RelationalOp` and the pipeline lint; Helland at the ledger claim
and `reentry`; Michie at `memo`; Saltzer & Schroeder and ECMA-262 at `isolation`;
Cockburn at `ports`; Meyer at the question/act split. A reader meets the source beside
the rule it justifies, not in a bibliography.

## The slices

1. Built: the effect level is the entity's. `#Effect.level?` left the chart grammar,
   `machineLint` reads the durability `shell.yaml` carries (the named entity's, or the
   region's) and the `refused` rule is total; one durability table, the terminal's.
2. Built. Review by consequence is in: a program claim carries `owed`, read off
   reach — a `tab` entity, its validations, its charts and the handlers only they name
   are machine-checked and owe no element — and the pairing's program-side query reads
   it. `machine` and `effect` are compared kinds: a mecha machine and an effect by their
   `ir`, a terminal chart as `<entity ir>-<field>` claimed once however many screens
   carry it. A missing `machine` element is an advisory until the compiler draws it;
   chess reports six, golaberto three, both green. The level is a derived field:
   `#Entity.level` from the durability table (pronto keeps a copy of the terminal's, a
   test holds them equal), `#MechaMachine.level` external where a state invokes.
   Built, second half: `write.ts` draws the card into every entity element ir.html
   carries (`ir-render.ts`: a generated region after the element's heading, `ir/card.css`
   linked once), with the entity's machines as D2 pictures inside it, each the `machine`
   element the bijection asks for and holding the `effect` it invokes — so the advisory
   became an error, and chess's seven charts are answered without an author drawing one.
   The charts reach the renderer as `chart` fact rows, so pronto imports nothing of the
   terminal's. The d2 binary is the app's: CI builds every app, golaberto and truco
   included, with the tree's `write.ts`, and installs only the app's `.mise.toml`, so
   the pin sits beside `buf` and `duckdb` in each app and in the released template.
   Every D2 node is a quoted key — a chart over a boolean field names its states `true`
   and `false`, which D2 reads as keywords. Each screen also carries its D2
   neighbourhood from its settled reads, and the app overview is the union of
   those graphs. The contract lives in [[../../SPEC|the artifact spec]] and
   [[../screens|screens]].
3. Built: the entity's face. Writers are derived with their operations; reads
   are `eager | lazy`; the parsed CEL owns `cel: {checks, enums, bounds}`. One
   terminal mutation vocabulary is narrowed by forms, charts and mecha;
   strict relational append is `create`. Effects carry `input`, a required
   non-empty `note`, plain-word `reentry`, and the invoking machine's derived
   entity. Units declare `compartment | realm | agent`, `ports: {reads, raises}`
   and, for an agent, a required unique-keyed memo performed by the host.
   Mounts and seats refuse undeclared ports; markup lint also refuses raises
   nobody handles. Chess's stockfish memo is `Answer` by `(fen, nodes, lines)`.
   Machine `initial` supplies the column's missing default; states supply its
   CHECK only without field CEL, closed enums admit every state, and seeds
   name states. Terminal charts carry no `context`: their initial row comes
   from literal field defaults, filter equalities and `initial`; explicit
   `data-empty-row` stays whole. Assembly screens require derived charts as
   they require reads and writes, with empty projections only for `markup`'s
   bootstrap. These contracts live in [[../../SPEC|the artifact spec]],
   [[../screens|screens]], [the binding reference](https://github.com/bonisoft3/omnishell/blob/v0.19.1/REFERENCE.md)
   and [the terminal's units](https://github.com/bonisoft3/omnishell/blob/v0.19.1/docs/terminal.md). `shape` and
   process isolation remain deferred with the exemptions-and-units split.

4. Built: SQL calls name a `sql` hatch, `notify`/`stream` are raises, and raw
   pipelines are hatches. The contract is [[../../SPEC|the artifact spec]].
5. Built: the trigger is Mecha's interpreter. Transition predicates remain
   guards; the retained-ledger upgrade installs `state.effectInterpreters`.
6. Built: markup lint and the terminal refuse executable markup; the door and

## Rejected

- **A flat `hatches.kind` enum** (`sql | proto | container`): it names three things with
  nothing in common but the word; the level and the record are what a reviewer needs.
- **An authored level** (`level: *derived | explicit`, with a mismatch check): it can never
  be legitimately set, so it would be a field whose only valid value is the one the
  compiler already knows — write-only noise, with one proven misuse behind it.
- **A `twice` field of the author's**: the answer is derivable everywhere but the hatch;
  `delivery` is derived, and the hatch's `note` is its reason.
- **`why` beside `note`**: two prose fields for one judgment.
- **ACID 2.0 as a second level 3**: the distinction is real and is one lint about place,
  not a rung on the axis.
- **Five algebras**: 1 and 2 are protocols (a single-writer register, OCC with abort);
  pretending otherwise would be false precision. Two algebras and an axis of ownership
  is both true and enough.
- **A fifth effect "with no guarantees"**: an effect without a ledger is what a `process`
  hatch already is; giving it the effect vocabulary would lose the one property that
  makes an effect replayable.
- **Code with a level of its own**: code describes; a level on code would let a handler
  claim what only the row it writes can decide.
- **The sink inline under the invoking state**: XState keeps the reference (`invoke.src`)
  apart from the implementation (`setup({actors})`); so do we, and the effect's name is
  the cluster-wide binding name besides.
- **`seed` renamed** (`initialState`, `fixtures`, `bootstrap`): `seed` is the database's
  word for rows that exist before anyone writes; XState's `initial` names a state and its
  `context` the data, neither of which these are.

## Prior art

Sagas (Garcia-Molina & Salem 1987; the compensatable / pivot / retriable classification);
ACID 2.0 (Helland & Campbell, *Building on Quicksand*, 2009; Helland, *Idempotence Is Not
a Medical Condition*, 2012); CRDTs (Shapiro et al. 2011), CALM (Hellerstein & Alvaro
2020), LVars; optimistic concurrency (Kung & Robinson 1981); durable execution and the
recorded side effect (Temporal); exactly-once sinks in stream processing (Flink,
Kafka); memo functions (Michie 1968); algebraic effects and handlers (Plotkin &
Pretnar) for "describe, then a handler performs", and effect theories for the laws;
ports and adapters (Cockburn 2005), Erlang ports, the WebAssembly component model's
worlds; protection domains (Saltzer & Schroeder 1975), object capabilities (Miller,
*Robust Composition*, 2006) and SES for the isolation rungs, ECMA-262's realms and
agents for their names; command–query separation (Meyer 1988) for questions and acts;
XState's actors and `input` for the shape of an effect.

## Open

- **What AML has that `#Entity` lacks** (noted, not decided). A `note` on every field,
  which the card has no column for today; `tags` (`pii`, `deprecated`) as metadata review
  by consequence could levy; nested attributes for a json column (golaberto's
  `payload_json string` is a column the card can say nothing about); polymorphic
  relations, needed by no app yet. And three static lints Azimutt runs that pronto could
  run by consequence: a `*_id` column without `ref`; same-named columns with different
  types across entities; a `ref` without an index. Its data-dependent rules (unused
  entities and indexes, growth, slow queries) belong to the measured side.
- **Inbound acts.** A peer calling us — an acquirer's webhook, a calendar's callback — is
  an act from the peer's side and a source from ours, keyed by the peer's event id and
  landing by `ensure` on a `server` row so the machine fires once however many times it
  is delivered. The taxonomy has the dual of `effects` for outbound and nothing declared
  for inbound: no door, no key, no ir kind. The Cal.com oracle's reminders are the first
  case that needs it.
- **Foreign embeds** (postponed). A `realm` is same-site by construction (its `src` resolves against
  the app); a third party's frame has its own origin and its own network, which is a
  `process`'s reach inside the browser. Either it is a hatch with an `egress` of one
  origin, or it is not offered.
- **Exemptions and units as two collections** (postponed). `capabilities.hatches` and
  `capabilities.vendored` cut across the taxonomy: `sql`, `proto` and a raw pipeline are
  inspection *exemptions* with no isolation at all, while a `process` is a *unit* with the
  widest. `exemptions: {sql, proto, pipeline}` and `units: {compartment … process}`, with
  `isolation: "process"` being what makes a unit a hatch, would make the grammar say what
  the tables here say.
- **A question's memo across tiers.** An `agent`'s memo is an entity; a cluster-side
  question (an LLM, an FX rate, a geocoder) has no declared home yet — the airlock's
  ledger is for acts, and a content-keyed memo wants its own table and its own hatch
  kind, the question's counterpart of `effects`.
- **Blobs.** The direction: a **content-addressed** blob is immutable and keyed by its own
  hash, so writing it twice is `x ⊔ x = x` by construction — a grow-only set, level 3,
  exactly-once with no coordination; a memo for bytes, as git's object store is. What makes
  a blob a hatch is being *named*: a mutable path someone overwrites is a last-writer-wins
  register the platform can neither validate nor replay, so `delivery: unspecified`. The
  rule that follows is that a blob is referenced from a row by its hash and never by a
  name, and garbage is what no row references: rows stay the lattice, the store is their
  memo. Durability then needs no new word; the blob plane's record owes the mechanism.
- **Delete on the feed.** Deleting twice is idempotent, but delete and insert do not
  commute; a replicated `delete` is a tombstone question (two-phase and observed-remove
  sets) the op vocabulary does not yet answer.
- **Tier-aware hatches.** A `process` hatch's `egress` is a compose network today; at k8s
  or Cloud Run it is a policy the deployment writes, and the record should say which.
- **The fence at the door.** CSP with Trusted Types breaks any library that builds HTML
  from strings; the terminal already forbids them, but a vendored `realm`'s content may
  not, and the header applies per origin.
